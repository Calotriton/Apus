      Pick a species to check its detections.

      Sets a confidence threshold for each species from the detections you checked, so that its detections are right as often as you ask.

      What it is. The lowest confidence score that a detection of one species needs to be kept.

      Why. The threshold in Settings is the same for every species, but a score does not mean the same for each. A score of 0.5 can be almost always right for one species and wrong half the time for another.

      How. From your checks, Apus works out how the share of correct detections grows with the score, and finds the score at which it reaches your target. With a target of 0.95, about 95 in 100 detections at the threshold are right, and more of those above it (Wood and Kahl 2024).

      What it costs. A higher threshold also drops correct detections: Correct kept shows how many of yours stay. Your checks tell how often detections are right, not how many calls were missed.

      Detections marked unsure are left out. If you listened to the audio clips outside Apus, clips you moved into folders named correct and incorrect, inside each species' clip folder, count too. A threshold holds for recordings like the ones you checked: the same sites, season and recorders.

      Measures how well the detections of Perch 2.0, the model Apus runs, match your own annotations: the share of detections that are right (precision), and the share of annotated sounds that were found (recall). It does this for single calls and for the species of each dataset (Funosas et al. 2026).

      Annotations
      A table of the sounds you annotated, with their species, start and end: CSV, text or Excel. One table for every recording, or one per recording.

      Columns. Once you choose the files, Apus shows their first rows and asks which column holds the species, the start and the end of each sound, and the recording. It guesses from the column names, in English or Spanish. Audacity labels, Raven selection tables and Excel workbooks work as they are.

      Times. In seconds from the start of the recording, such as 12.5 or 12,5, or as minutes and seconds, such as 1:02.5.

      Recordings. Without a recording column, each file is one recording, and its name starts with the recording's name: rec01.txt or rec01.Table.1.selections.txt for rec01.wav. A row with a recording but no species, or an empty file, means you listened and heard nothing.

      Species names. Scientific names or eBird codes. For other names, such as common names, add a synonyms file in Settings.

      Annotate everything. A detection counts as right when its 5 s window overlaps an annotation of the same species in the same recording. Annotate every species you hear, or the detections of the ones you skipped count as false.

            Detection

            The model gives every species a confidence score from 0 to 1 in each 5 s window. A detection is a score at or above this threshold. A lower threshold finds more calls, but also more false detections. A score is not a probability, and the same score means different things for different species. To set a threshold per species, check some detections and use the Thresholds tab.

            The lowest confidence score (0 to 1) that counts as a detection

            The model listens to 5 s windows. With a 5 s hop the windows follow each other. With 2.5 s they overlap by half: a call cut by a window's edge is heard whole in the next one, but the run takes twice as long, and a call in two windows is detected twice.

            The model listens to 5 s windows

            How many of the best-scoring species are kept for each window, before the threshold. It must be at least 1 divided by the threshold (10 for 0.10), or a species above the threshold could be cut. Apus raises it when needed.
